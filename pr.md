# Product Requirements: Clipping Agency Landing Page

## 1. Project Overview

Build a high-converting single-page landing website for a clipping/content-distribution agency.

The website should position the company as a **content distribution system**, not simply a video editing service.

The primary objective is to make a creator, personal brand, podcast host, or business understand:

> "I already have long-form content. This agency can turn that content into a large amount of short-form content and distribute it at scale."

The website should communicate scale, credibility, speed, and modern internet culture while remaining visually polished and easy to understand.

---

https://clippingagency.co/

* Information hierarchy

# 7. Landing Page Structure

The page should generally follow this narrative:

1. Hero
2. Social proof / credibility
3. Results/statistics
4. Problem
5. Solution
6. How the system works
7. What happens to one piece of content
8. Distribution/platform section
9. Creator/client proof
10. Process
11. Why the agency
12. FAQ
13. Final CTA
14. Footer

The exact ordering may change if a better conversion structure is discovered during implementation.

---

# 8. Hero Section

The hero must communicate the business in approximately 5 seconds.

Required components:

### Eyebrow

Something short such as:

"THE CONTENT DISTRIBUTION ENGINE"

### Main headline

The headline should focus on the outcome, not the service.

Possible direction:

> Turn One Video Into Millions of Views.

or:

> Your Content Deserves More Than One Upload.

or:

> Turn Your Long-Form Content Into a Distribution Engine.

The AI may generate alternatives, but the final headline must communicate:

CONTENT → DISTRIBUTION → SCALE

### Supporting copy

Explain that the agency converts long-form content into short-form clips and distributes them across relevant social platforms.

### CTA

Primary CTA should be immediately visible.

### Hero visual

Potential visual directions:

* Video timeline
* Multiple clips branching from one source video
* Social media distribution network
* Content nodes
* Stacked vertical videos
* Motion-blurred content cards
* Creator video → clipping system → multiple platforms

The visual should communicate **multiplication**, not simply "video editing."

# 11. Problem Section

The problem should be framed around **distribution**, not content quality.

Core idea:

> Great content is useless if nobody sees it.

Possible problems:

* One piece of content gets posted once
* The creator relies on one account
* Valuable moments remain buried inside long videos
* Editing capacity limits output
* Posting manually is slow
* Content distribution stops after the original upload
* Creators spend time creating instead of distributing

The section should create the realization:

> "I already have enough content. I have a distribution problem."

---

# 12. Solution Section

Introduce the clipping system.

Explain:

1. Client provides long-form content.
2. Strong moments are identified.
3. Clips are edited for short-form platforms.
4. Clips are reviewed.
5. Content is distributed through the campaign.
6. Performance is tracked.
7. Winning formats inform future content.

The system should feel repeatable and scalable.

# 17. How It Works

Use a simple 3–4 step process.

Example:

### 01 — Send Your Content

Give us your podcasts, interviews, streams, videos, or existing content.

### 02 — We Find the Moments

Our clipping workflow identifies moments worth turning into short-form content.

### 03 — We Distribute

The resulting clips are formatted and distributed through the campaign.

### 04 — We Optimize

Performance data is used to improve future content and distribution.

Keep this section extremely easy to scan.

# 19. Visual Direction

The website should feel:

* Modern
* Bold
* Internet-native
* Premium
* Slightly aggressive
* Performance-oriented
* Technological without becoming a generic SaaS website

Avoid:

* Generic corporate blue
* Generic stock photos
* Excessive gradients
* Random 3D objects
* Overused glassmorphism
* Template-looking cards
* Excessive rounded corners
* Decorative elements with no purpose

---

# 20. Color Direction

The reference website can be used as the structural baseline, but the new website should establish its own visual identity.

Start with:

* Dark base
* Strong primary accent
* High-contrast typography
* Controlled secondary accent
* Neutral supporting tones

The exact palette should be selected based on the brand.

Use color primarily to establish hierarchy.

Do not add colors simply because the design feels empty.

---

# 21. Texture Direction

Textures may be used to make the design feel more editorial and distinctive.

Possible textures:

* Grain
* Noise
* Subtle scanlines
* Paper texture
* Compression artifacts
* Video-frame textures
* Digital distortion
* Subtle grid systems

Texture must remain subordinate to content.

Rule:

> If removing the texture makes the design clearer, the texture is too strong.

AI-generated textures are allowed where appropriate.

---

# 22. Typography

Typography should create hierarchy rather than decoration.

Use:

* Large display typography for major statements
* Strong weight contrast
* Short paragraphs
* Tight headlines
* Comfortable body-text width
* Clear CTA typography

Avoid using too many fonts.

Prefer:

* 1 display family
* 1 supporting family

or one highly flexible family.

---

# 23. Motion

Motion should reinforce the idea of distribution.

Good motion ideas:

* Clips multiplying
* Content cards moving outward
* Subtle marquee
* Number counters
* Video previews
* Scroll-triggered reveals
* Distribution lines
* Platform cards appearing from one source

Avoid:

* Excessive animations
* Slow cinematic transitions everywhere
* Animations that delay understanding
* Motion purely for decoration

Performance is more important than showing off.

---

# 24. Responsive Design

The page must work on:

* Desktop
* Laptop
* Tablet
* Mobile

Mobile should not simply be a compressed desktop layout.

Explicitly redesign:

* Hero composition
* Navigation
* Statistics
* Cards
* Case studies
* CTA sections
* Large visual compositions

Ensure no horizontal overflow.

---

# 25. Content Rules

All copy should be:

* Short
* Direct
* Confident
* Outcome-oriented
* Easy to scan

Avoid:

* Long corporate paragraphs
* Generic marketing jargon
* Empty claims
* Repeated explanations
* Artificially sophisticated language

Use concrete language.

Bad:

> "We leverage innovative omnichannel ecosystems to unlock unprecedented content synergies."

Good:

> "We turn long-form videos into short-form clips and distribute them at scale."

---

# 26. Claims & Accuracy

This is a critical requirement.

The AI must distinguish between:

### Confirmed facts

Information explicitly provided by the client or verified from approved assets.

### Proposed copy

Marketing language that does not introduce factual claims.

### Unverified claims

Information that needs client confirmation.

When information is uncertain, use:

`[VERIFY]`

or

`[CLIENT TO CONFIRM]`

Never silently turn assumptions into facts.

---

# 27. Success Criteria

The final website succeeds if a visitor can answer these questions quickly:

1. What does this company do?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why does it matter?
4. What happens after I contact them?
5. What proof exists?
6. Why should I trust the company?
7. What should I do next?

If the visitor cannot answer these questions without reading the entire page, the page has failed.

---

# 28. Final Product Principle

The website should not feel like:

> "A video editing agency with a fancy website."

It should feel like:

> "A content distribution operation that happens to use clipping as its engine."

The central idea of the entire page is:

**CONTENT → CLIPPING → DISTRIBUTION → SCALE**
